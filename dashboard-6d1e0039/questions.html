Questions that must be answered before deciding

These are the unknowns that actually change the recommendation. Each one is listed with why it matters and how to close it — an email, a phone call, or a page that has to be read at booking time.

QuestionWhy it changes the decisionHow to close itStatus
Is each candidate event a freezeout, and can the poker room put that in writing?No official document on the Strip says “no re-entry”, which is the core of the brief. Everything else is a comparison between events that might allow a second buy-in.Email or call the poker room; ask for the event terms, not the marketing page.open
Does the planned trip include a weekend?Saturday/Sunday carries the biggest guarantees (Wynn $1M flights, Venetian $500K) but also the highest hotel rates - the Venetian night of Dec 5 is $779.25 versus $231.75 on Dec 6.Set exact dates; the calendar grid on the booking engine shows per-night prices before you commit.open
What is the maximum acceptable cost per tournament hour?A $600 one-day event with 40-minute levels runs roughly 10-12 hours; a $200 turbo runs 5-6. Cost per hour, not buy-in, separates the two.Use the trip calculator on this site and sort by cost per level.open
Will the player take the Bellagio $40 chip-gratuity or Caesars $50 add-on?Both buy an advantage; if yes, those venues belong in the plan and the strict list must be described as a preference, not a constraint.Decide before comparing; mixing them makes the ranking meaningless.open
Which hotel tier is required - Venetian suite or a standard Wynn king?Same 7 nights: Venetian $6,927.21 all-in versus Wynn's $711 average room rate before tax. Venetian includes a 650 sq ft suite; Wynn includes self-parking.Book refundable rates on both and compare after tax at checkout.open

What happens if a day's primary event is a multi-flight bag?Bagging Day 1 forces attendance on Day 2, which can collide with the next day's primary event.Prefer one-day events as primaries and keep flights as conditionals.open
