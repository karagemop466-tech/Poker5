Suggestions — pick a path before changing anything

Improvements, ordered only by effort and likely impact, so you can choose. Nothing here is implemented yet; the site stays as verified as it is until you pick.

IDChangeEffort Impact
G01Publish a per-event cost-per-hour column
Buy-in alone hides the difference between a 20-minute-level turbo and a 40-minute structure. Deriving hours from levels × level length and dividing buy-in by hours gives a fair comparison. The dashboard already stores level length for every row, so this is a display change only.
SHigh
G02Ask the rooms for written freezeout confirmations in one batch
A single email template to Venetian, Wynn, Bellagio, Caesars and Resorts World asking “is this event single-entry, and what is the late-registration cut-off” would convert the largest block of unverified rows in one pass.
SHigh
G03Track the Venice/Wynn structure PDFs by checksum
Both rooms update their structure PDFs in place. The scheduled worker already hashes each URL; surfacing the hash change date next to each event would show exactly when a rule changed.
SMedium
G04Add a like-for-like hotel comparison at one length of stay
Seven nights, two adults, one room is the current baseline. Adding 3- and 5-night variants at the same check-in date makes the weekend premium visible instead of averaged away.
MHigh
G05Model the bag risk for multi-flight primaries
If a Day 1 is bagged, the next day is committed. A simple flag that a chosen primary has a Day 2 on the following day would stop the planner proposing an impossible sequence.
MMedium
G06Add an off-Strip section
The 8 a.m. goal and the no-advantage-purchase rule both point off-Strip, where rooms publish true freezeouts more often. This is the single largest coverage gap.
LHigh

Effort S = a display or data change; M = a new computation or page; L = new research scope.

The one change that unlocks the most

Everything on this site is capped by one missing fact: no Strip room publishes a freezeout rule. One batch of emails asking “is this event single-entry, and when does registration close” would convert 46 unverified Venetian rows and dozens of Wynn rows into a real single-entry list (suggestion G02). Until then, ranking those rows as if they were single-entry would be exactly the kind of inference the brief forbids.
