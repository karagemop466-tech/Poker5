Executive summary

  • The strict list is empty on purpose. All 37 Venetian structure sheets and every Wynn terms sheet the WPT posted were read. Not one event says “no re-entry” or “freezeout”. Two Venetian events publish a re-entry price (#18 and #58, “All subsequent buy in's after the initial buy in will be $200”) and every Wynn event with a posted terms sheet states unlimited re-entry. So no Strip event can be certified single-entry today — the candidates stay marked unverified instead of being promoted.
  • Best published value among the one-day Venetian events (guarantee ÷ buy-in, descriptive only, not a forecast): #18 $200 → $20,000 (100.0×), #58 $200 → $20,000 (100.0×), #36 $800 → $75,000 (93.8×), #54 $600 → $50,000 (83.3×). These are the rows where the house is guaranteeing the most money per dollar of entry.
  • Dates matter more than buy-in. The Venetian's own booking engine prices the same Luxury King suite at $231.75 on Sun Dec 6 and $779.25 on Sat Dec 5. A seven-night Dec 2–9 stay is $6,927.21 all-in (room $5,837.25 + resort fees $385.00 + tax $817.46), an $872.82 nightly average including the resort fee but excluding tax.
  • There is no 8:00 a.m. start on the Strip. The earliest verified start is 9:10 a.m. (Venetian #46, Dec 12); regular slates begin 10:10 a.m. (Venetian), 10:00 a.m. (Wynn Super Gold Million), 9:00 a.m. (Horseshoe). An all-day plan therefore runs roughly 9:10 a.m. → midnight.
  • Two Strip rooms cannot be used under the brief. Bellagio sells a $40 “voluntary staff gratuity” that adds 20,000 chips and Caesars Palace sells a $50 add-on for 15,000 chips — both are pay-for-advantage. They are documented separately, not ranked.
