Why is the strict single-entry list empty?

The event-specific official rules reviewed either expressly permit repeat entry (WPT Prime, Caesars Palace daily events, Wynn daily re-entry formats, and Bellagio Friday/Sunday), leave the entry cap out (several Venetian one-day structure sheets), or describe multi-flight rules that do not settle what happens after a bust. The dashboard does not infer “one entry only” from silence. See the screening table.

Does “re-entry not listed” mean there is no re-entry?

No. It means the sources in this snapshot do not establish the rule. A tournament director could confirm a rule that is not published online, but that would need to be verified before the event belongs on the strict list. Those items are labeled Entry rule unverified.

Is a rebuy or add-on the same as a re-entry?

No. A re-entry is another tournament entry after elimination (or a later flight entry, subject to event rules). A rebuy buys chips while still in the event. An add-on buys chips at a set point, often regardless of stack. They are different terms, but all may increase outlay; this dashboard labels each type separately where the source discloses it.

Why is the Dec 9 checkout day still on the tournament calendar?

The hotel quote window is check-in Wed Dec 2 and check-out Wed Dec 9: seven nights. The tournament calendar includes both endpoint dates so the selected research range is visible. Events starting Dec 9 are shown for context only; playing them would usually require a later checkout or an additional night.

Does the schedule include any 8:00 a.m. starts?

No 8:00 a.m. start appears in the selected official Venetian/WPT calendars. The earliest scheduled start in this dated series list is 10:10 a.m. at The Venetian. Caesars Palace’s separate daily flyer advertises 10 a.m., 2 p.m., 6 p.m. and 9 p.m. events. This does not establish that any tournament can be completed before another begins.

Can I play two tournaments on one day?

The calendar sorts starts, not finishes. It does not have reliable end times, and multi-day events continue beyond their first day. Same-time starts at two properties plainly conflict; a later start may also overlap a long earlier event. Treat it as a discovery tool, not a guaranteed playing itinerary.

Are the hotel prices all-in and comparable?

The Venetian booking interface displayed a $7,054.77 seven-night estimate including $385 in resort fees and $832.52 in taxes for two adults, but also showed a maximum-capacity / 4+ guest filter warning. Wynn showed a rounded $711 average nightly rate for a Resort King, including the resort fee but excluding taxes. The dashboard estimates Wynn taxes separately using the current 13.38% Strip lodging tax; it is not a booking-engine total. Compare only directionally and reconfirm room availability and final totals.

Are the walk times room-to-poker-room?

No. Casino Compass provides secondary casino-to-casino walking estimates, such as Venetian–Wynn at 10–15 minutes. Official property pages place The Venetian poker room at The Palazzo, Level 2 of Grand Canal Shoppes, and Wynn’s room on the Encore casino floor. No exact route from a particular hotel room to a poker seat has been timed; internal navigation and crowds can add time. See Hotels & routes.

How should a source conflict be handled?

Follow the most detailed governing document only when it clearly applies. When the schedule, structure and event page disagree, the row is marked for review instead of choosing the most convenient interpretation. The WPT Dec 5 Day 2 line and the Dec 6 Mystery Bounty turbo label are examples; Bellagio Saturday is another. See Flagged for Review.

Source hierarchy

How a fact earns a label.

Confirmed

Official venue schedule or event-specific governing rules say it plainly.

Unverified

A relevant official source exists but leaves the requested rule or availability unstated.

Review

Two official sources conflict, a term is stale, or a booking UI gives a warning inconsistent with a quote.

External links are verified for source identity and HTTPS format in the local build; the project’s automated check does not make live HTTP requests to those sites.
